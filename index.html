Five small habits that make your chess better

  1. Look at your opponent's last move. Before planning your own idea, ask what changed on the board.
  2. Check for hanging pieces. A quick scan for unprotected pieces can prevent a lot of frustrating mistakes.
  3. Develop with a purpose. Try to bring your knights and bishops into useful squares instead of moving the same piece repeatedly.
  4. Keep your king safe. Castling is often a sensible way to move your king away from the center and connect your rooks.
  5. Review one moment afterward. You don't have to analyze the whole game. Find one decision you would make differently next time.

None of these habits requires expensive tools. A consistent approach, a little patience, and regular games can make a real difference. If you're a beginner, focus on enjoying the process rather than worrying about every result.
